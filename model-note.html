Performance & Variance Copilot

ECON 449 · Use case 27 · Phase 2 · Wamiq Ahmed

Purpose and decision

Predict next month's country revenue in EUR, compare its landing estimate with next-month budget, and flag deviations strictly greater than ±5%. The dashboard shows this next to December's actual rule; January forecasts are country totals across all brands/channels. Existing descriptive pages remain available, with new Segments and Model pages.

Data, relationships and features

3,888 synthetic monthly P&L rows, 2020–2025, six countries, three brands and three channels. All 2023–2025 Phase 1 figures are preserved. Earlier history extends the same seasonality, gradual growth, country demand offsets, segment pricing, unit deviations and FX relationships. Ridge learns from country, log-revenue lags 1/2/12, past-only 3/12-month means, calendar sine/cosine, elapsed time, lagged FX and its latest change. Target-month actual revenue, units, costs, bridge effects and realised FX are excluded. Latest observed FX is carried forward; next-month budget is known planning information.

Evaluation against simple baselines

Eligible 2021–2024 observations train; January–June 2025 selects Ridge alpha 0.01 by MAE. July–December 2025 is reserved and evaluated once, without shuffling. Coefficients stay fixed during 36 sequential one-month-ahead test predictions; each preceding month's observations are then available. The design and data hashes are saved before testing. A separate production fit uses all history for January 2026.

Segments and actions

Standardised trailing-year price/volume/mix/FX contributions relative to budget feed K-means. June 2025 profiles select k=2: silhouette 0.262 versus 0.214 for k=3 and 0.122 for k=4; larger choices create singleton groups and progressively reduce inertia. Two groups balance separation and actionable size. Latest profiles are assigned to the frozen centres.

Drivers, errors and limits

Lag history, calendar seasonality and trend explain the smooth synthetic patterns; coefficients on correlated lags are associations, not causal findings. Data lacks unexpected demand shocks and inherited EUR-market FX variation is illustrative. Strong dummy-data accuracy does not establish real-world accuracy. Six countries make segments exploratory; membership can change over time. No calibrated uncertainty interval is supplied. The flag is a threshold on revenue regression, so a classification probability threshold does not apply.
